02 · STRATEGY GUIDE

One market, three lenses

The scanner identifies context before interpreting any signal.

01

Quant Ensemble

Combines trend, relative strength, mean reversion, breakout quality, risk, and fundamental valuation. A Monte Carlo stress test then checks target-versus-stop odds and scales position size.

02

Value + Momentum

Combines trailing P/E, forward P/E, PEG, earnings growth, and price momentum. Missing or negative-earnings ratios reduce data quality rather than becoming automatic buy signals.

03

Trend Confirmation

Uses moving-average structure, slopes, and 3-, 6-, and 12-month momentum. SPY-relative performance prevents confusing a broad rally with stock-specific strength.

04

Bollinger + RSI Reversal

Requires band re-entry and exhaustion, then blocks bullish reversals during severe long-term downtrends.

05

ATR Breakout

Uses prior support or resistance, an ATR buffer, close location, and abnormal volume. Today's bar remains excluded from its threshold.

06 · RESEARCH LIBRARY

Evidence behind the system

A growing, plain-English record of the research used to design Stock Strategy Lab. Every source includes the idea, its limits, and how it influenced the app.

View research log ↓
CORE PRINCIPLE

Estimate odds—not certainty

Professional systematic investing combines weak evidence, controls exposure, and expects individual forecasts to be wrong. The ensemble score is evidence strength, not the probability that a stock will rise.

VALIDATION PRINCIPLE

Protect against false discoveries

Chronological testing, next-session execution, trading costs, adequate samples, and out-of-sample evaluation matter more than impressive historical returns.

RISK PRINCIPLE

Survival comes first

Volatility, drawdown, concentration, and market regime determine how much confidence a directional signal deserves.

MOMENTUM2012

Time Series Momentum

Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen

Documents momentum across equity-index, currency, commodity, and bond futures. Past direction contained useful information across multiple horizons.

Used in the app: 3-, 6-, and 12-month momentum, moving-average structure, and trend slopes.

Read the paper ↗
TAIL RISK2016

Momentum Crashes

Daniel & Moskowitz

Shows that momentum can suffer severe losses during abrupt market reversals, especially after declines and during high volatility.

Used in the app: regime-dependent weights, volatility penalties, drawdown measures, and reduced confidence when risk is elevated.

Read the paper ↗
RISK MANAGEMENT2017

Volatility-Managed Portfolios

Moreira & Muir

Studies reducing portfolio exposure when volatility is high and reports improved risk-adjusted performance across several factors.

Used in the app: annualized realized volatility, risk-quality scoring, and moderate/elevated/high risk labels.

Read the paper ↗
RELATIVE VALUE2006

Pairs Trading

Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst

Tests a systematic relative-value strategy that matches historically similar stocks and trades unusually large divergences.

Used now: the principle of comparing a stock with a benchmark. Future: sector and peer-relative models.

Read the paper ↗
TRANSACTION COSTS2009

Dynamic Trading with Predictable Returns and Transaction Costs

Gârleanu & Pedersen

Explains how optimal trading depends on expected returns, changing opportunities, risk, and the cost of adjusting a portfolio.

Used in the app: turnover matters, and the backtest subtracts an estimated cost on every entry and exit.

Read the paper ↗
FACTOR TIMING2020

Factor Timing

Haddad, Kozak & Santosh

Studies time variation in factor opportunities and the potential value of conditioning factor exposure on the market environment.

Used in the app: bull, bear, volatile, and range regimes receive different ensemble weights.

Read the paper ↗
VALIDATION2025

Interpretable Hypothesis-Driven Trading

Deep, Deep & Lamptey

Presents a walk-forward framework emphasizing information discipline, rolling out-of-sample testing, realistic constraints, and interpretable hypotheses.

Used now: plain-English reasons and next-session execution. Future: rolling walk-forward result panels.

Read the paper ↗
OVERFITTING2026

Spurious Predictability in Financial Machine Learning

Nikolopoulos

Shows how repeated model and specification searches can generate apparently significant results even where genuine predictability is absent.

Used in the app: transparent fixed rules, data-quality warnings, sample-size warnings, and no black-box price prediction.

Read the paper ↗
VALUATION2006

Valuation Approaches and Metrics

Aswath Damodaran

Explains how valuation multiples depend on growth, risk, payout, and interest rates—and why PEG can mislead when definitions or comparison groups are inconsistent.

Used in the app: P/E, forward P/E, and PEG are combined with growth and momentum; missing or negative ratios are not labeled cheap.

Read the paper ↗
FUNDAMENTAL MOMENTUM2015

Fundamentally, Momentum is Fundamental Momentum

Robert Novy-Marx

Finds that momentum in company fundamentals and earnings information helps explain the performance and risks of price-momentum strategies.

Used in the app: earnings-growth estimates influence valuation, but a favorable multiple still requires acceptable price momentum.

Read the paper ↗
PAPER TRADING2025

Investopedia Stock Simulator

Investopedia Simulator Support

Documents a risk-free virtual environment supporting stocks, ETFs, options, short sales, portfolio tracking, games, and familiar trade controls.

Used in the app: recommendations become reviewable limit-order tickets that the user manually approves and enters into Investopedia.

Read the documentation ↗
POSITION SIZING2025

Risk per Trade

Investopedia educational reference

Explains calculating shares from account risk and the distance between entry and stop rather than choosing an arbitrary share count.

Used in the app: each proposed order risks at most 1% of a $100,000 virtual account and cannot exceed a 10% position.

Read the reference ↗
MONTE CARLO RISK2026

Drawdown Risk Beyond Brownian Motion

Francesco Landolfi

Shows that Gaussian simulations can misstate drawdown risk when returns have fat tails, volatility clustering, skewness, or persistence. Multiple path-dependent risk measures are needed.

Used in the app: 5,000 historical block-bootstrap paths, target-before-stop ordering, tail outcomes, and explicit model limitations instead of a single predicted price.

Read the paper ↗

What this evidence does—and does not—prove

  • Published historical patterns can weaken after discovery or disappear after costs.
  • A strategy that works across assets or portfolios may not work on one stock.
  • Backtests are estimates, not live results, and depend on execution assumptions.
  • The current app uses daily OHLCV data; it does not yet include fundamentals, options, news, alternative data, or portfolio constraints.
  • No paper or signal can guarantee a profitable investment.
